# Titan Orbit — YouTube intro script

A first-look video you can **read while you play**. Target length: **14–18 minutes**. Comfortable range: 10–20.

Print this, or keep it on a second monitor. Speak the lines. Do the **[SHOW]** notes.

---

## Before you hit record

Set up a match where you can actually demo things. Ideal:

- You are already in a game (skip the main-menu stare).
- Your home planet and at least one asteroid field are close.
- A **neutral** planet is reachable.
- You have (or can quickly get) enough gems to open the orbit station.
- You know a **map edge** so you can wrap on camera.
- If possible: a second player, or bots / another team, so combat is real.

Record at 1080p or 1440p. Game audio up. Mic a little hotter than the engines.

If a section is messy live, keep talking and come back to it. The script is a path, not a prison.

**Talking pace:** one short line, then a breath, then fly. Do not rush the wrap or the first dock — those are the “wow” shots.

# The script

Spoken lines are in normal text.  
**[SHOW]** = what the camera should be doing.  
*(whisper)* = optional aside if you have time.

---

## 1. Hook — start already flying  
**~40 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Do not start on a menu. You are already in space. Full thrust. A planet in frame. Shoot once so the guns speak.

Welcome to Titan Orbit.

This is not a slow empire builder.  
This is a team fight for an entire map — and the map does not have an edge.

**[SHOW]** Point the nose at the seam. Fly off the screen.

If I leave this side…

**[SHOW]** Hold the wrap. Let the camera snap. Do not talk over the first half-second of the jump.

…I am already on the other side.

That is the whole fantasy.  
One sky. No walls. Your team versus theirs.

Let’s play it.

---

## 2. What this game actually is  
**~60 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Click the minimap. Press **M**. Full map. Hover a few planets. Collapse it again.

Here is the game in one sentence.

You and your team fly ships.  
You mine gems.  
You grow planets.  
You move people.  
And you win when every planet on the map is yours.

Up to five teams.  
Red, blue, green, gold, purple.

**[SHOW]** Press **Tab** so Team A through E flash on the leaderboard.

This is arcade-fast.  
But it is also a war.  
A good miner feeds a good fighter.  
A good transport takes the next world.

If you only shoot, you stall.  
If you only mine, you get erased.  
The fun is doing all of it in the same ship.

---

## 3. How you fly  
**~90 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Empty space. Slow down. Teach the camera like a friend is sitting next to you.

Flying is simple on purpose.

Mouse aims the ship.  
Right mouse is the throttle.  
Hold it — you go.  
Let go — you coast.

Left mouse is the gun.

**[SHOW]** Aim, thrust, fire. One clean pass.

**Ctrl** is Space Brakes.  
On — you slow down when you are not thrusting.  
Off — you float forever, like a real ship.

**[SHOW]** Toggle Ctrl. Drift. Toggle it back on. Stop clean.

**Shift** plus thrust is Overdrive.  
That is the “I need to be there now” button.

**[SHOW]** Burst toward a planet. Cut it before you look sloppy.

Your ship has weight.  
Health, gems, people — they make you heavier.  
A full cargo hold does not turn like an empty one.  
That is on purpose. Greed has a cost.

*(whisper)* Do not lecture the HUD. Glance at the speedometer if it is on screen, then fly again.

---

## 4. The doughnut map  
**~70 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Find the edge again. This time slower. Then do it a second time in another direction.

Most space games have a border.  
A wall. A fade. A “you cannot go there.”

Titan Orbit is a torus.  
Think of a doughnut.  
Or Pac-Man, if Pac-Man had planets.

Fly off the right… you come in from the left.  
Fly off the top… you come in from the bottom.

**[SHOW]** Wrap twice. Let it land.

Here is the clever part.

The game does not pretend those are two different places.  
A planet on this edge still owns a ship that wrapped to the other side.  
Ranges, orbits, beams — they take the short way around.

**[SHOW]** If you can, sit near a planet on one edge and show a ship or moon “across” the seam.

So the map feels huge.  
And it still feels like one battlefield.

Once you feel that, you never want a wall again.

---

## 5. Mining — the heartbeat  
**~90 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Asteroid field. Ram, grind, or shoot rocks. Gems pop. Fly through them. Tractor beam pulls stragglers.

You need gems.

Gems are money, progress, and greed in one glowing pile.

Asteroids are the mines.  
Fly in. Break them. Scoop the drop.

**[SHOW]** Fill the hold. Watch the gem count climb.

Your ship has a tractor beam.  
It pulls gems toward you so you are not playing vacuum-cleaner simulator.

Hold **V** if you need to dump cargo.  
Sometimes you dump to get light again.  
Sometimes you dump so a teammate can grab it.  
Sometimes you dump because an enemy is about to delete you and you refuse to donate your wallet.

**[SHOW]** Dump a few. Pick them back up. Keep it playful.

Do not sit in the rocks forever.  
A full hold that never makes it home is just a loot pinata.

Which brings us to the best feeling in the game.

---

## 6. Orbit, moons, and the station  
**~2 minutes**

**[SHOW]** Approach your home planet. Stop thrusting in the ring. Let the ship catch the orbit.

See this ring around the planet?

If I coast in it — no throttle — the ship catches the orbit.  
It is like slipping into a current.

**[SHOW]** Ride the ring for a few seconds. Do not talk over the first catch.

Thrust or fire and you break out.  
You are always in control.  
The orbit is a gift, not a trap.

Around friendly worlds you will see moons.  
Those moons are your banks. Your shops. Your hangar.

**[SHOW]** Dock. Let the landing cinematic play. Open the orbit station.

When you dock, two things happen.

One: you deposit gems.  
That is how planets grow.  
That is how your team gets richer.

Two: you open the Orbit Station.  
This is the loadout screen. The heart of the game.

**[SHOW]** Slow pan across the station. Do not click randomly. Point as you talk.

Ship parts.  
Cards.  
The upgrade tree.  
Rockets. Mines. Drones.

The station is not a pause menu from another genre.  
It is a pit stop in a war.

Buy what you need.  
Get out.  
The map is still moving.

---

## 7. Upgrades — make *your* ship  
**~2.5 minutes**

**[SHOW]** Stay docked. Speak slower here. Viewers need to see the UI.

You do not unlock a new ship from a dusty crate.  
You build one.

First: the ship itself.

There is an upgrade tree.  
Levels one through six are the family line — bigger hull, more teeth, more cargo.  
Level seven is the MEGA.  
A unique monster. One of those on a team changes the conversation.

**[SHOW]** Hover the tree. If you can afford a step, buy it. If not, hover and describe.

You can also spin cards.  
Cards are the spicy part.  
More firepower. Better tractor. Tougher drones. Weird bonuses.

**[SHOW]** Open the card shop. Spin one if you can. React to whatever you get.

And then there are the parts.

Each family is a kit.  
Cockpit, engines, wings, guns.  
You swap pieces. You stack stats.  
Offense, defense, energy, mobility, cargo — you can read it on the bars.

**[SHOW]** Hover a part. Show the stat chip. Buy something visible — a gun or an engine.

Here is the rule that keeps it fair.

The planet you are docked at caps what you can buy.  
A hot ship on a baby world cannot shop like a king.  
Level the planet. Then the shop grows with you.

That is why dumping gems at home matters.  
You are not just shopping for yourself.  
You are unlocking the next era for the whole team.

---

## 8. Planet ships — a dozen personalities  
**~75 seconds**

**[SHOW]** If you can visit a second owned or captured world, do it. If not, open the map and point at different planets.

Every planet sells its own ships.

Astro Eagle. Cosmic Shark. Hyper Falcon. Light Fox.  
Galaxy Raptor. Meteor Mantis. Night Aye. Force Badger.  
Star Force. Proton Legacy. Space Excalibur. Strider Ox.

**[SHOW]** Say the names of the ones you can actually show. Skip the rest.

They do not just look different.  
They fly different.  
They tank different.  
They mine different.

Your home world starts you in its family.  
Capture a new world — that world’s hangar opens.

So the map is not only territory.  
It is a catalog.

The team that takes more planets does not just own more dots.  
They own more toys.

---

## 9. People — how you actually take a planet  
**~2 minutes**

**[SHOW]** Friendly planet with population. Load people. Fly toward a neutral. If combat will happen, lean into it.

Gems pay for power.  
People take the land.

On a friendly world you pick up troops.  
Your hull can only carry so many.  
Upgrade that if you want to be the bus.

**[SHOW]** Load. Show the people count. Leave.

Then you fly those people to a planet that is not yours.

Neutral worlds are the easy first bite.  
Enemy worlds are the war.

**[SHOW]** Launch the transport. Watch the yellow pods. Let them land.

Those little transports are not decoration.  
They are the invasion.

You need more people on the ground than the planet already has.  
One more. That is the swing.

When it flips — it is yours.

**[SHOW]** Capture if you can. Celebrate. If you cannot finish it, say so and show the attempt.

A captured world grows population.  
It can grow levels.  
It gets defenses.

**[SHOW]** Point at a turret pad if one is visible.

Those guns are planetary defense.  
They shoot enemy ships.  
They shoot incoming transports.  
They even shoot asteroids that get in the way.

You can take the turret yourself.  
Sit in the gun.  
Defend the sky.

**[SHOW]** If you can possess a turret, do it for ten seconds. Right-click thrust to get out. Do not get stuck in the tutorial.

This is the real loop.

Mine.  
Upgrade.  
Move people.  
Take the next world.  
Repeat until the map is one color.

---

## 10. Combat — when someone says no  
**~2 minutes**

**[SHOW]** Find a fight, or pick a dummy target / asteroid if the lobby is empty. Be honest if you are demoing on rocks.

You will not take the map in silence.

Left click is your main gun.  
Press **B** to cycle the fire type.  
Laser. Plasma. Whatever your hull is packing.

**[SHOW]** Burst fire. Cycle B once. Fire the new type.

Rockets and mines live on the left of the screen.

**Up** and **Down** pick the pack.  
**Alt** uses it.

Caret on a rocket — Alt launches it.  
Caret on a mine — Alt drops it.

**[SHOW]** One rocket. One mine if you have them. Do not dump the whole inventory.

Mines are rude in the best way.  
Park them on a lane.  
Park them on a wrap.  
People forget the doughnut has choke points.

Ramming is a weapon too.  
This is a physics game.  
A heavy hull at speed is a statement.

**[SHOW]** If it is safe, bump a rock. Do not suicide into a teammate.

Die and you are not out.  
You pick a friendly planet and come back.

**[SHOW]** If you die naturally, talk through the respawn map. If not, just say it.

Death is a delay.  
Not a lecture.

---

## 11. Drones, comms, and the war room  
**~90 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Back at a station if you can buy a drone. Otherwise point at ones already flying.

You do not have to do every job with your own hands.

The store sells drones.

Fighter drones hunt.  
Mining drones work the rocks.  
Shield drones eat bullets so you do not.

They buy at the level of you and the planet — whichever is lower.  
Again: grow the world, grow the toys.

**[SHOW]** Buy one if you can. Watch it deploy.

Team play needs a voice that does not require a keyboard essay.

Hold **S**.

**[SHOW]** Open comms. Tap a short callout. Attack. Defend. Thanks. GG.

Three words. On your hull. Your team sees it.

It is sports chat for people who are also steering a missile.

**[SHOW]** Press **M**. Full map again. Point at territory, homes, connections.

The minimap is the war room.

Your team. Their team.  
Planets. Asteroids. You.

Click it. Expand it.  
This is how you stop playing “the planet in front of my nose” and start playing the whole doughnut.

**Tab** flips the leaderboard through every team.  
Who is winning the map.  
Who is just loud.

---

## 12. How you win  
**~60 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Full map. Speak like a closer.

The win condition is not a score.  
It is not a timer, even if a clock is on screen.

You win when your team owns every planet that can be owned.

Not most.  
All of them.

That means the last world is a knife fight.  
Everyone left alive piles onto one rock.  
Transports. Turrets. MEGAs. Panic.

That is the video I want to make next.  
A full war, start to finish.

Today you needed the language.

Fly.  
Wrap.  
Mine.  
Dock.  
Build.  
Move people.  
Take the sky.

## 13. Close  
**~45 seconds**

**[SHOW]** Pretty shot. Orbit a planet. Or wrap one last time and hold the pose.

That is Titan Orbit.

A team space game that is fast in the hands and huge in the head.  
A map with no edge.  
A ship that is yours because you built it.  
A galaxy that changes color when your team is better.

If you want more, tell me in the comments.

A full match.  
A MEGA showcase.  
A “how to not feed the other team your gems” guide.

I am going to go take one more planet.

**[SHOW]** Thrust. Music up. Cut on motion — not on a menu.

See you in orbit.

# Edit notes (for you, after the take)

- Keep the first wrap in the first 30 seconds of the *finished* video, even if you move other chapters.
- Cut dead air in the station. Keep the first dock landing — it looks expensive.
- If combat is empty, do not fake a war. Say “lobby is quiet” and demo guns on rocks, then promise a war video.
- If you stumble a name, leave it. Perfect pronunciation is less trustworthy than a laugh.
- Loudest music under the hook and the closer. Dip it when you teach controls.
- Target cut: **15:00**. If you are under 12, you skipped a showcase. If you are over 20, you lingered in the shop.
